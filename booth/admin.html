How it talks

Visitors are standing up in a loud hall. Shorter answers get listened to; long ones get walked away from.

Measured, not guessed: the Arabic voice speaks about two words per second.

Greets once per visitor, not once per answer. Turn this off if the stand is busy and you want answers only.

Time from the question ending to the first word being available to speak, measured against this knowledge base. Haiku is genuinely about a second quicker. It is not the default because on the same three Arabic questions it ran 62, 32 and 19 words against a 22-word budget, and wrote "11,000" in digits where the answer should say the number in words — which an Arabic voice can read wrong out loud. Listen to a few answers on it before an event, not during one.

Turning the avatar on and off

A live avatar session costs something on every renderer — money, or one of the limited number of sessions your account can hold open at once. These two settings decide how much of the day the stand spends paying for a screen nobody is standing at.

On, the booth opens asleep and the whole panel is the start button. Off, it connects the moment the page loads and holds the session all day — only worth it for a demo where a face already on screen when someone walks in is the effect you are showing. The booth can still answer typed questions while asleep; it wakes itself first.

Separate from the conversation reset above, and normally shorter: the face should go before the words do, because the face is the part that costs. Reconnecting takes a few seconds and is paid while the next visitor is walking up. Raise it if the stand is busy enough that it reconnects constantly.

Saved per renderer — these controls always edit whichever one is selected at the top of this page, because the vendors do not send the same shape of video. Anam sends a tall portrait, which wants Fill the stage. Simli sends a 512×512 square, and filling a portrait stage with a square crops the head on every side — so it wants Fit the whole frame, and the bars at the edges are the stage's own background rather than a fault. Zoom scales after that; the anchor is the point held still while it scales, so set it on the face.
