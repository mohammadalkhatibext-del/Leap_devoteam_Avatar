# Agentic Avatar for the Devoteam Booth at LEAP: A 3-Day Build Plan, Vendor Comparison, and Pricing

## TL;DR
- Build a composable pipeline, not an all-in-one platform: use HeyGen LiveAvatar (Lite mode, about $0.10/min) or Anam AI as the photorealistic rendering layer, drive it with your own Claude-based RAG orchestrator plus a Gulf-quality Arabic TTS (Azure ar-SA or ElevenLabs), and wire it together with LiveKit Agents, which has official plugins for every major avatar vendor and first-class support for AI coding agents like Claude Code.
- Arabic is the single biggest technical risk. HeyGen's and most vendors' native "interactive" language menus do not include Arabic, so you must go "bring your own ASR/TTS" (HeyGen Lite mode, Tavus echo mode, or Anam/Beyond Presence audio passthrough). Deepgram Nova-3 (17 Arabic variants, under 300ms, up to ~40% lower word error rate than competitors) and ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime (3.1% WER on the Arabic FLEURS benchmark, 150ms latency) are the most reliable real-time Arabic STT; Azure ar-SA neural voices (Hamed/Zariyah) or ElevenLabs give the best Gulf/MSA TTS. Whisper-based ASR fails on Arabic and should be avoided.
- Two vendors let you prototype in parallel within hours and are fully self-serve (no sales call, which is a hard blocker under a 3-day deadline): HeyGen LiveAvatar and Anam AI, both with official LiveKit plugins and starter repos. Tavus and Beyond Presence are strong self-serve backups. Avoid anything gated behind enterprise sales (DeepBrain kiosk, Soul Machines, UneeQ) and avoid fully self-hosted open source (MuseTalk/Wav2Lip) for a 3-day timeline.

## Key Findings

**The category splits cleanly into "rendering-only" vs "all-in-one."** Rendering-only APIs (Simli, HeyGen LiveAvatar Lite, Anam passthrough, Tavus echo) take your audio and return a lip-synced video stream, letting you own the STT/LLM/TTS and therefore own Arabic quality and Devoteam RAG grounding. All-in-one platforms (Tavus CVI full mode, Beyond Presence managed agent, HeyGen Full mode) bundle STT+LLM+TTS+avatar for faster setup but weaker Arabic control. For a Devoteam-grounded, Arabic-critical kiosk, the rendering-only path wins.

**Real-time two-way conversation is genuinely available and self-serve from several vendors.** HeyGen LiveAvatar, Anam, Tavus CVI, Beyond Presence, and Simli all offer real-time WebRTC streaming with sub-second to ~1.5s end-to-end latency and are self-serve. Synthesia's "Interactive Avatars" is closed beta (disqualified). Soul Machines, UneeQ, and DeepBrain's kiosk product are enterprise-sales-gated.

**Arabic is not a first-class citizen in the "interactive" menus.** HeyGen users report the Interactive Avatar language dropdown does not list Arabic; the workaround is Lite mode with your own Arabic ASR/TTS. Tavus does list Arabic natively (43 languages). Anam claims 70+ languages, Beyond Presence "dozens." But in every case the actual Arabic voice quality is inherited from the underlying TTS (ElevenLabs, Cartesia, or Azure), so you get the best result by explicitly routing a Gulf-tuned TTS.

## Details

### 1. Interactive / streaming avatar vendors

**HeyGen LiveAvatar (Interactive Avatar) — RECOMMENDED PRIMARY.** Real-time streaming avatar, a separate product from HeyGen's video-generation studio, with its own login at app.liveavatar.com. Two modes: Full mode (HeyGen handles LLM/voice/avatar, 2 credits/min) and Lite mode (you bring your own LLM/ASR/TTS, HeyGen provides only the avatar layer, 1 credit/min). Standalone LiveAvatar pricing: Free (test only: ~5 min Full / ~10 min Lite, 2-min session cap, 1 concurrency); Starter $19/mo for 150 credits (~75 min Full / ~150 min Lite, i.e. ~$0.25/$0.13 per min, 5-min session cap, 5 concurrent, 720p); Essential $99/mo for 1,000 credits (~500 min Full / ~1,000 min Lite = $0.20 / $0.10 per min, 20-min session, 20 concurrent); Business $475/mo for 5,000 credits (~$0.19 Full / ~$0.095 Lite per min, 60-min session, 40 concurrent, includes one custom 1080p avatar, full API); Enterprise custom with a stated minimum budget of $48,800. Base credit rate: 1 credit = 10 US cents = 30 sec Full / 1 min Lite. Language in LiveAvatar depends on the connected LLM and Voice ID; HeyGen exposes voice IDs from ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Azure, and Google, so Arabic works via Lite mode with an Arabic voice ID. Official LiveKit plugin (livekit-plugins-liveavatar) and starter repo (github.com/heygen-com/liveavatar-starter-livekit-agent-python). Self-serve for all tiers except Enterprise. This is the best combination of photorealism, self-serve access, BYO Arabic, and Claude Code friendliness.

**Anam AI — RECOMMENDED PARALLEL PROTOTYPE.** API-first real-time avatar over WebRTC, CARA model, ~180ms avatar response latency, claims top rank on an avatar benchmark. Supports 70+ languages for speech recognition (its own pages are inconsistent, 50+ vs 70+); voices from Cartesia Sonic and ElevenLabs Flash v2.5. Crucially supports "audio passthrough" mode: you stream raw PCM audio from your own TTS and Anam only does lip-sync, so you fully control Arabic and RAG. Effective pricing works out to roughly $0.15-0.20/min (~$9-12/hour); overage rates advertised around $0.11-0.16/min; there is a free tier. Clean official LiveKit plugin (livekit.plugins.anam) with a ~10-line integration, plus an official ElevenLabs-agent-plus-avatar integration and Stream Vision Agents integration. Excellent Claude Code target. Note: session caps and monthly minute expiry make it better for attended booth use than always-on.

**Tavus (CVI, Phoenix-4) — STRONG BACKUP.** Purpose-built conversational video with proprietary perception (Raven), turn-taking (Sparrow), and rendering (Phoenix-4) models. Tavus states Sparrow collapses latency to around 600ms and the Phoenix-4 pipeline achieves sub-600ms end-to-end conversational latency, though its docs publish an SLA target of under one second utterance-to-utterance; measure in your own setup. Natively supports 43 spoken languages including Arabic (ar) via tavus-auto TTS routing, with Azure as a bring-your-own-key fallback (East US region). "Echo mode" bypasses the LLM so you can feed your own text/audio (i.e., your Claude RAG output). Pricing: Free (25 CVI min, 1 concurrent, no custom replica); Starter $59/mo (100 min, 3 replica trainings, 3 concurrent); Growth $397/mo (500 min, 7 replicas, 15 concurrent, conversation recording); Enterprise custom. Overage $0.32-0.37/min. Custom replica from a ~2-minute video, training ~30 min; extra replicas $40-65 each. 30-second minimum charge per conversation, billed in 6-second increments. Image-to-Replica lets you build a replica from a single photo. Official LiveKit and Pipecat plugins. Caveat: multiple reviewers describe cloned-replica voice as somewhat robotic; validate Arabic voice quality before committing.

**Beyond Presence (bey.dev) — STRONG BACKUP.** Developer-focused real-time avatars; ~250ms speech-to-video, ~1s end-to-end. Speech-to-Video API (feed any audio) plus Managed Agent API (bring your own OpenAI-compatible LLM; custom TTS on roadmap). Managed-agent pricing is credit-based, 1 credit = 1 minute; plans from around $49/mo with a free trial. Voice synthesis via ElevenLabs; "dozens" of languages including Arabic per third-party listings (its own docs only say "dozens"). GDPR-compliant, EU-developed. Official LiveKit plugin, plus Python/TypeScript SDKs, Pipecat and n8n integrations on GitHub (bey-dev). Good Claude Code target.

**Simli — viable rendering-only option.** Infrastructure-first "face layer for AI agents," sub-300ms visual latency, pay-as-you-go around $0.009-0.05/min depending on source (its own materials cite the lower end; expect ~$0.009/min at volume), $10 free credits. Custom avatar from a single image. Uses WebRTC on Daily infrastructure; LiveKit and Pipecat support; JS and Python SDKs plus a no-code widget. BYO LLM and TTS (so Arabic is fully in your control). Cheapest per-minute renderer, but face quality is a notch below Anam/HeyGen/Tavus; good for a low-cost parallel test.

**D-ID (Agents API) — viable but pricier.** Pioneer in talking-head; Agents SDK and Agents Streams over WebRTC with knowledge/RAG and custom-LLM support (specify your own LLM base URL). Real-time agent streaming is positioned as an enterprise-grade, high-API-usage feature; API cost is often cited around $5.90/min for streaming, which is far more expensive than the renderer-only vendors. Supports Arabic among 100+ languages but voice quality is tier-dependent. Self-serve Studio plans exist (Lite from ~$4.70-5.90/mo), but real-time at scale gets costly. Reasonable backup, not the primary.

**Synthesia — DISQUALIFIED for this use case.** Best-in-class for pre-rendered training video (240+ stock avatars, 140+ languages including Arabic, custom digital twins ~$1,000/yr on Enterprise), but its avatars "read scripts, they don't listen." Its real-time "Interactive Avatars" (a LiveKit plugin) is explicitly closed beta and requires an early-access form, so it cannot be relied on in 3 days. Keep Synthesia only as the source of a pre-rendered fallback loop video.

**DeepBrain AI (AI Human / AI Kiosk) — enterprise-gated.** One of the few vendors with a purpose-built real-time AI kiosk product (retail/banking receptionists), Arabic TTS available, studio-quality custom avatars. But the AI Human / kiosk and interactive-avatar API are sales-led; public self-serve pricing is for the AI Studios video generator, not the real-time kiosk. The custom "Studio Avatar" requires a physical shoot. Enterprise sales cycle kills the 3-day timeline.

**Soul Machines, UneeQ, Ravatar, Hour One, Yepic, Colossyan, Hedra.** Soul Machines and UneeQ are premium "digital human" platforms aimed at enterprise deployments with sales-led onboarding (disqualified on timeline). Ravatar is a Middle-East-active digital-human/kiosk integrator, also sales-led. Hour One and Colossyan are pre-rendered video tools (not real-time). Yepic offers real-time avatars but has a smaller ecosystem. Hedra is listed as deprecated in LiveKit's plugin catalog and its live quality was rated low; do not use.

**ElevenLabs Agents (as the voice brain, paired with a video layer).** Not a video avatar itself, but a strong conversational-voice engine that officially pairs with a video layer. Conversational AI starts at 10 cents/min (8 cents/min on annual Business), excluding LLM cost; third parties cite $0.08-0.12/min by model tier. Full Arabic support: Scribe v2 Realtime ASR delivers state-of-the-art accuracy in over 90 languages with 150ms latency and a 3.1% word error rate on the Arabic FLEURS benchmark (5.5% on Common Voice), outperforming Google Gemini and OpenAI Whisper; Multilingual/Flash v2.5 TTS supports Arabic (Saudi Arabia, UAE). Officially wired into HeyGen LiveAvatar (Lite/PCM 24000 Hz), Anam (Cara, sub-1s), and Beyond Presence (default voice synthesis). A very good "voice + brain" option if you prefer ElevenLabs orchestration over rolling your own with Claude, though note LLM grounding/guardrails are then somewhat less flexible than a pure Claude pipeline.

**OpenAI Realtime API + avatar renderer.** Viable architecture (LiveKit has a plugin), but the OpenAI Realtime voice pipeline is English-centric and gives you less Arabic control than an explicit Deepgram-plus-Azure/ElevenLabs chain. Use Claude for text reasoning/RAG and a dedicated Arabic STT/TTS instead.

**Open-source real-time lip-sync (MuseTalk, Wav2Lip, LivePortrait, SadTalker, Duix, OpenAvatarChat).** MuseTalk (Tencent, MIT license) does real-time lip-sync at 30+ fps on a single data-center GPU (V100), 256x256 mouth region, and is the strongest open option; LivePortrait animates a still portrait. Realistically NOT achievable to production quality in 3 days: you own the GPU provisioning, WebRTC integration, warm-start, autoscaling, barge-in, and Arabic pipeline yourself, and quality is visibly below the commercial renderers. Skip for the event; revisit only for a later data-residency-driven self-host.

### 2. Architecture options (ranked by effort vs. quality)

**Option A — All-in-one managed agent (fastest, least control).** Tavus CVI full mode or Beyond Presence managed agent: the vendor hosts STT+LLM+TTS+avatar; you upload a system prompt and (for Beyond Presence) your own LLM/knowledge. 3-day timeline: comfortably achievable, working demo in Day 1. Risk: Arabic voice quality is whatever the vendor's TTS gives; RAG grounding and guardrails are shallower; you may not hit the exact Devoteam brand voice. Good as a backup path.

**Option B — Vendor avatar rendering layer + your own orchestration (RECOMMENDED).** HeyGen LiveAvatar Lite or Anam passthrough or Tavus echo, driven by your own pipeline: Claude for reasoning and RAG over Devoteam docs with strict guardrails, plus your chosen Arabic STT/TTS. You push text or audio to the avatar stream. 3-day timeline: achievable with LiveKit Agents doing the plumbing. Risk: integration glue and turn-taking/barge-in tuning; mitigated by official starter repos. This is the best quality-vs-effort trade-off and the one to build first.

**Option C — Fully composable pipeline via LiveKit Agents or Pipecat.** LiveKit Agents (Python) orchestrating Deepgram (Arabic STT) + Claude (LLM/RAG) + Azure or ElevenLabs (Arabic TTS) + an avatar plugin (Anam, HeyGen LiveAvatar, Tavus, Beyond Presence, Simli). This is effectively Option B expressed in LiveKit's framework and is the concrete implementation path. LiveKit provides Silero VAD for turn detection, a multilingual turn detector, and enhanced noise cancellation (important in a loud hall), plus an `lk docs` CLI and AGENTS.md file explicitly designed for coding agents. 3-day timeline: realistic. Risk: the noise-cancellation plugin is LiveKit-Cloud-only; plan connectivity accordingly.

**Option D — Fully self-hosted open source (MuseTalk etc.).** Highest control and data residency, no per-minute fee, but you own GPUs and all real-time engineering. 3-day timeline: not realistic. Do not attempt for LEAP.

### 3. RAG / knowledge grounding layer

For a fixed, modest corpus of Devoteam documents, do NOT stand up a vector database under a 3-day deadline unless the corpus is large. Two fast, robust approaches:

- **Long-context stuffing with Claude plus the Citations API (recommended).** Anthropic's Citations API launched in January 2025 and is generally available on the Anthropic API and Google Cloud's Vertex AI, and (as of June 30, 2025) on Amazon Bedrock. You pass Devoteam documents as document content blocks with `citations.enabled = true`; Claude then grounds each claim in the source text and returns the exact cited passages. Critically for cost, Anthropic confirms that `cited_text` does not count toward your output tokens, so you do not pay for the quoted source text returned. This is the single fastest way to get grounded, low-hallucination answers with source traceability. Combined with prompt caching, it is cheap and low-latency for a booth-sized corpus.
- **Managed RAG if the corpus is large or changes often:** OpenAI Assistants file search, Vertex AI Search, Azure AI Search (also gives you Saudi/UAE data-region options), Pinecone or Supabase pgvector, LlamaCloud, Vectara, Ragie, or no-code CustomGPT.ai / Chatbase. For Arabic retrieval quality, use multilingual embeddings (e.g., Cohere multilingual or OpenAI text-embedding-3-large) and test retrieval on real Arabic queries; naive English-only embeddings degrade Arabic recall.

**Guardrail strategy (keep the avatar strictly on Devoteam):**
- System prompt: "You are the Devoteam booth assistant at LEAP. Answer ONLY using the provided Devoteam documents. If the answer is not in the documents, say you don't have that information and offer to connect the visitor with a Devoteam representative. Never invent services, client names, pricing, or numbers. Do not discuss competitors, politics, religion, or any topic unrelated to Devoteam."
- Require citations for every factual claim (Citations API) and refuse when no citation is available.
- Add a lightweight topical classifier or a pre-LLM regex/keyword gate to short-circuit obviously off-topic input.
- Cap answer length and set a conservative temperature.
- Log unanswered questions so staff can follow up.

### 4. Hardware and venue setup

- **Microphone:** In a loud hall, open-mic wake-word recognition will misfire constantly. Use push-to-talk (a large physical button or on-screen tap) as the primary interaction model; this is the single most reliable noise-handling choice. Pair it with a directional/cardioid or short shotgun mic aimed at the speaking position, or a handheld/gooseneck mic mounted at the kiosk. Kiosk-industry guidance is explicit that microphone quality and noise cancellation are the leading cause of failed voice-kiosk deployments, so test the exact mic in a noisy environment beforehand. Barge-in/interruption: enable VAD-based interruption (Silero via LiveKit) but gate it behind push-to-talk so ambient noise doesn't trigger it.
- **Directional speaker:** Use a directional/parametric speaker (e.g., Focusonics, Audfly, Akoustic Arts) above the screen so the avatar's voice is audible to the visitor without adding to hall noise or bleeding into staff conversations.
- **Screen:** A 55-to-75-inch commercial display at counter/eye height, portrait orientation if you want a life-size single-person avatar.
- **Machine:** A mini PC or a solid laptop (recent CPU, 16-32GB RAM) running the browser client is sufficient because all heavy rendering is done in the vendor cloud; you are just receiving a WebRTC video stream. Run Chrome/Edge in fullscreen kiosk mode (`--kiosk`), disable sleep/screensaver, and auto-relaunch on crash.
- **Session reset between visitors:** Add an idle timeout (e.g., 20-30s of silence) that clears conversation history and returns to an attract-loop, plus an explicit "New visitor / start over" button. This prevents context bleed and protects privacy.
- **Network:** Conference Wi-Fi is unreliable. Bring a dedicated 5G router with a local Saudi SIM/eSIM (STC, Mobily, or Zain) as primary or backup, and hardwire Ethernet if the venue offers it. Budget for ~1.5-2.5 MB/s upstream per active avatar stream.
- **Fallback plan (critical):** If the live avatar or network fails, auto-fall back to (1) a pre-rendered looping Devoteam avatar video (produced in Synthesia or HeyGen studio), and (2) a text-chat interface on the same screen backed by the same Claude RAG endpoint. LiveKit-based apps can be coded so that if the avatar session fails to initialize, the conversation continues in voice-plus-text mode automatically.

### 6. Legal / compliance / regional considerations

- **Likeness and consent:** If you use a real Devoteam employee's face, get written, signed consent covering AI replica creation, commercial use at a public event, and data retention/deletion. Tavus and others build a consent-script step into replica creation; keep that record. A stock avatar avoids the issue entirely and is the safer 3-day choice.
- **AI disclosure:** SDAIA's Generative AI Guidelines (2024) emphasize content authenticity and disclosure. Label the kiosk clearly as an AI assistant (in Arabic and English) and, where practical, follow watermarking/authenticity norms.
- **PDPL / data residency:** Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL, Royal Decree M/19), enforced by SDAIA, has been in full enforcement since the one-year grace period expired on September 14, 2024. It applies extraterritorially to Saudi residents' data and carries fines up to SAR 5,000,000 per breach, which the competent court may double for repeat violations; SDAIA had issued 48 enforcement decisions as of early 2026. If visitor audio is processed, minimize and avoid storing it: process speech in memory, do not retain recordings, and post a short privacy notice at the booth. SDAIA's November 2025 AI Adoption Framework adds governance expectations (transparency, human oversight, risk management). Prefer vendors/regions that let you keep processing in-region or that offer zero data retention (Azure has UAE/Saudi regions; LiveKit and several vendors offer configurable retention). Have Devoteam's DPO review before the event.
- **Vendor terms:** Confirm each vendor's terms permit commercial use at a live public event and that your plan tier includes commercial/white-label rights (HeyGen Business, Tavus Enterprise for full white-label, Beyond Presence commercial tiers).

### 7. Practical recommendation and day-by-day plan

**Primary path:** LiveKit Agents (Python) + Deepgram Nova-3 (Arabic/English STT) + Claude (RAG over Devoteam docs via Citations API, with strict guardrails) + Azure ar-SA or ElevenLabs (Arabic/English TTS) + HeyGen LiveAvatar (Lite mode) as the photorealistic renderer. Rationale: fully self-serve, cheapest per-minute, best Arabic control, official starter repo, and Claude Code can work directly from LiveKit's `lk docs` CLI and AGENTS.md.

**Backup path:** Same LiveKit orchestration but swap the avatar plugin to Anam (fastest 10-minute integration and best latency/photorealism) or Tavus (native Arabic, echo mode). Because the avatar is a swappable plugin, you can keep two renderers configured and switch with one line if one underperforms on Arabic lip-sync.

**Day 1 — Plumbing and one working English loop.** Clone the LiveKit agent-starter-python and the HeyGen liveavatar-starter-livekit-agent-python repos. Get a stock avatar talking in English end-to-end (push-to-talk, STT, a trivial LLM reply, TTS, avatar video in browser). Stand up the Claude RAG endpoint with the Devoteam docs and Citations API; test grounding and refusals in text first. In parallel, spin up the Anam quickstart as the second renderer.

**Day 2 — Arabic and grounding.** Switch STT to Deepgram Nova-3 Arabic (17 Arabic variants, under 300ms) and TTS to Azure ar-SA (Hamed/Zariyah) or an ElevenLabs Arabic voice; validate Arabic ASR accuracy and lip-sync on real Gulf-dialect test phrases. Wire the Claude RAG guardrails into the live pipeline. Tune turn-taking, barge-in, and the 20-30s session reset. Add the Devoteam branding (background, logo, colors, brand voice in the system prompt). Run the same Arabic test on both HeyGen and Anam and pick the winner on lip-sync and voice quality.

**Day 3 — Hardening and venue dress rehearsal.** Add the pre-rendered fallback loop and text-chat fallback. Configure fullscreen kiosk mode, auto-relaunch, and the 5G backup router. Load-test concurrency and a full 6-hour soak. Do a noisy-environment mic test with the directional mic and push-to-talk. Freeze the build, document the runbook, and rehearse the failure drills (kill Wi-Fi, kill avatar session, confirm graceful fallback).

## Recommendations
1. **Start today by provisioning three self-serve accounts in parallel:** HeyGen LiveAvatar (Essential $99), Anam (free/entry), and Tavus (Starter $59). This is the only way to compare Arabic lip-sync on your actual Devoteam content within the deadline. Do not wait on any vendor that requires a sales call.
2. **Build Option B/C first** (LiveKit + Claude RAG + Deepgram + Azure/ElevenLabs + HeyGen LiveAvatar Lite). Treat Anam as the hot backup renderer behind the same orchestration.
3. **Lock Arabic quality by Day 2.** If neither HeyGen Lite nor Anam gives acceptable Gulf lip-sync/voice, fall back to Tavus native Arabic or route Azure ar-SA through echo/passthrough. If Arabic ASR is the weak point, compare Deepgram Nova-3 against ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Realtime and, if needed, a Saudi-focused STT (e.g., Munsit) on your real test phrases.
4. **Use a stock avatar, not an employee likeness,** unless you can complete signed consent quickly; it removes the biggest legal and timeline risk.
5. **Ship the fallback loop and text chat before you ship polish.** A booth that never goes blank beats a slightly prettier one that crashes.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If measured Arabic STT word error rate is too high for your test phrases, switch STT provider before spending more on avatar tuning. If end-to-end latency exceeds ~2 seconds and feels broken, drop to a lighter renderer (Simli) or reduce video resolution. If venue Wi-Fi upload is below ~2 Mbps and 5G is congested, pre-stage the pre-rendered fallback as the default and use live avatar only when the network is healthy. If any preferred vendor turns out to gate the streaming API behind a sales call, drop it immediately and use the self-serve alternative.

HeyGen LiveAvatar, Tavus, and ElevenLabs pricing is best verified against their own pages the day you buy.
- Some Arabic-support and dialect claims (especially for Beyond Presence and Saudi-focused TTS vendors) come from third-party aggregators or the vendors' own marketing; benchmark independently on real Gulf-dialect audio before trusting them. Deepgram Nova-3's "up to ~40% lower WER" and ElevenLabs Scribe's "3.1% FLEURS WER" are vendor-published figures.
- Latency figures are vendor-published or model-card claims and move quickly; measure end-to-end in your own setup.
- Open-source self-hosting is deliberately excluded from the recommended paths due to the 3-day constraint, not because it lacks merit.
